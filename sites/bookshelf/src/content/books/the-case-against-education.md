## Chapter One: The Magic of Education

> The Ivory Tower Ignores the Real World

The disconnect between curriculum and job market has a banal explanation: Teachers, teach what they know. The question to answer is **_why_** employers care a lot about grades and diplomas. Employers throughout the economy defer their hiring decisions to the opinions of professors, throughout the hiring process.

> 💡 If schools boost students' income by teaching useful jobs skills, why do they entrust students' education to people so detached from the real world?

### The Basics of Signaling

> An important example is education as a signal of high individual productivity in the labor market. It is not necessary for education to have intrinsic value. Costly investment in education as such signals high ability.

**Signaling models have three basic elements:**

- First, there must be different types of people.
  - These people can differ in intelligence, conscientiousness, conformity, etc.
- Second, an individual's type must be non-obvious.
- Third, types must visibly on average.
  - In technical terms: Deviations from average are okay. A signal doesn't have to be definitive, just better than nothing.

### What does Education Signal?

From the employer's perspective, there is no cheap way to directly measure conformity. Education signals a _package_ of socially desirable strengths:

- **Intelligence**
  - The intelligent worker learns quickly and deeply.
- **Conscientiousness**
  - The conscientious worker labors until the job's done.
- **Conformity**
  - The **Model Worker,** obtains a degree, thus showing that you are able to look, talk, and act as such.

> Fantastic workers might have weak education credentials because; their family is poor; they're averse to student debt; they couldn't wait to start "doing things in the real world."

**Employers are running businesses, not logic classes. The hiring process is about prudence, not proof.**

#### "Locked-In" Syndrome

If you want the labor market to recognize your strengths, and most of the people who share your strengths hold a credential, you'd better earn one too. And, even when alternatives to traditional schooling already exist, these alternatives send employers the wrong message.

#### How Social Norms Contribute

Social norms take the naturally strong connection between education success and career success and make it unnaturally strong:

1. Employers notice the link between success at school and success at work, so they use education as a gatekeeper of the labor market. If good un-credentialed workers are sufficiently rare, employers ignore their very existence.
2. Talented, motivated people notice education's gatekeeping role, so they devote themselves to educational success in order to fulfill their career ambitions.
3. The frequency of talented, motivated people without a strong academic record falls, tightening the link between success at school and success at work.
