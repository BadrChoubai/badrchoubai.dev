## Chapter One: Embrace Reality and Deal with it

> Good principles are effective ways of dealing with reality. To learn your own: spend a lot of time reflecting.

Look to the patterns of those things that affect you in order to understand the cause-effect relationships that drive them.

There is nothing more important than understanding how reality works and how to deal with it. Emotions can help you to make better decisions; reconcile your emotions with your logic and only act when they are aligned.

### Be a Hyperrealist

**People who create great things aren't idle dreamers:** They are totally grounded in reality.

### Dreams + Reality + Determination = A Successful Life

To achieve success and drive process one needs to build a deep understanding of the cause-effect relationships that govern reality and have principles for using them to get what they want. **Being ungrounded in reality creates problems, not progress.**

### Truth is the essential foundation for any good outcome

**Be radically open-minded and radically transparent:**

- Radical open-mindedness and radical transparency are invaluable for rapid learning and effective change
  - Learning is the product of a continuous real-time feedback loop in which we make decisions, see their outcomes, and improve our understanding of reality as a result.
    Radical open-mindedness enhances the efficiency of those feedback loops: It makes what your are doing and why so clear to yourself and others that there can't be any misunderstandings

**Don't let fears of what others think of you stand in your way:**

- Learning to be radically transparent opens up the possibility for a more efficient world; Instead of people hiding what they think, they would share it openly.

**Embracing radical truth and radical transparency will bring more meaningful work and more meaningful realtionships.**

- This takes practice and changing one’s habits; It will take awhile—18 months—but that's how long it takes to change most habits.

### Look to Nature

(**to learn how reality works)**

**All the laws of reality were given to us by nature.** To learn how reality works: spend time in nature, reflect on how it works by observing, reading about, and learning from specialists about the subject. **Nature is smarter than you; let it teach you how reality works.**

#### Man's most distinctive quality is our singular ability to look down on reality from a higher perspective and synthesize an understanding of it

But our higher-level thinking can also make us confused: This causes people to confuse what they want to be true with what is actually true.

When trying to understand anything, one can approach the subject with two perspectives:

1. Top Down: A focus on learning the genetic code of all species.
2. Bottom Up: Learning the DNA sequences that make ducks different from other species.

Seeing things from the top down is the best way to understand ourselves and the laws of reality within the context of overarching universal laws. But to understand the world accurately you need both perspectives.

**Don't get hung up on your views of how things "should" be because you will miss out on learning how they really are.**

- Typically, people's conflicting beliefs or conflicting interests make them unable to see things through another's eyes.
- Nature seems to define good as: What's good for the whole, and optimizes for it.

**To be "good" something must operate consistently with the laws of reality and contribute to the evolution of the while.**

**Evolution is the single greatest force in the universe; it is the only thing that is permanent and it drives everything.**

- It is the process of adaptation that generally moves things toward improvement; It is the process by which things either adapt and improve or die.
- Fail, learn, and improve quickly.

### Evolving is life's greatest accomplishments and its greatest reward

One of the great marvels of nature is how the whole system can create a beautifully operating and evolving whole.

It has produced:

- Incentives and interactions that lead to individuals pursuing their own interests and resulting in the advancement of the whole
- The natural selection process
- Rapid experimentation and adaptation.

**The individual's incentives must be aligned with the group's goals.**

**Reality is optimizing for the whole—not for you.**

- Contribute to the whole and you will likely be rewarded.

**Adaptation through rapid trial and error is invaluable.**

- Natural selection's trial-and-error process allows improvement without anyone understanding or guiding it. The same can apply to how we learn:
  - Memory-based learning
  - Subconscious learning
  - "Learning" that occurs without thinking at all.

**Realize that your are simultaneously everything and nothing—and decide what you want to be.**

- Our lifetimes: 1/3,000 of humanity's existence; which is only 1/20,000 of the Earth's existence.

**What you will be will depend on the perspective you have.**

### Understand nature's practical lessons

Understanding how nature and evolution work can be helpful in many ways. It helps you frame your reality more effectively in making difficult choices. When you look at reality through the perspective of how it really works, you should realize that the things that seem "bad" to you are only that way because of the preconceived notions of what you want. Instead, put what you're reacting to in the context of the fact that reality is built to optimize for the whole rather than for the "I".

**Maximize your evolution.**

- The things we strive for in life are just the bait. Chasing after them forces us to evolve, and it is that evolution (of ourselves, and others) that is the most rewarding.
- For most people success is struggling and evolving as effectively as possible: Learning rapidly about oneself and one's environment, and then changing to improve it.
- The need to have meaningful work is connected to man's innate desire to improve. And relationships are the natural connections to others that make us relevant to each other and to society more broadly.

**Remember _"No pain, no gain"_**

- Everything nature has made has a purpose, so nature gave us pain for a purpose.

**It is a fundamental law of nature that in order to gain strength one has to push one's limits, which is painful.**

- This is true whether we are talking about building the body or the mind. And especially true when people confront the harsh realities of their own imperfections.

### Pain + Reflection = Progress

**There is no avoiding pain, especially if you're going after ambitious goals.** If you can develop a reflexive reaction to psychic pain that causes your to reflect on it rather than avoid it, it will lead to your rapid learning/evolving. It's much more effective to face painful realities that are caused by your problems, mistakes, and weaknesses, you should not want to operate any other way. It's just a matter of building the habit of reflecting on them.

**Reflect well while you're in pain, reflect after that pain passes.** The challenges you face will test and strengthen you. If you're not failing, you're not pushing your limits, and if you're not pushing your limits, you're not maximizing your potential.

The higher you ascend, the more effective you become at working with reality to shape outcomes towards your goals.

**Go to the pain rather than avoid it.**

- **Don't let up on yourself, become comfortable always operating with some level of pain. You will evolve at a faster pace.**
- Choose healthy and painful truth, not comfortable delusion.
- Pain is the signal, like switching from not exercising to exercising, developing the habit of embracing the pain and learning from it will "get you to the other side."
  - Identifying, accepting, and learning how to deal with your weaknesses.
  - preferring that the people around you be honest with you rather than keep their negative thoughts about you to themselves.
  - Being yourself rather than having to pretend to be strong where you are weak.

**Embrace tough love.**

- Don't be embarrassed about your problems, recognize that everyone has them. Bringing them to the surface will help you break your bad habits and develop goods one, and you will acquire real strengths and justifiable optimism.
- This evolutionary process of productive adaptation and ascent also pertains to how individuals react to (inevitable) setbacks. Something will get you, you will be in pain and think to yourself: "I don't have the strength to go on." You almost always do, however; your ultimate success will depend on you realizing that fact.
- **The faster one appropriately adapts, the better.** If you do this well, you can change your psychological reaction so that what is painful can become something you crave.

### Weigh second- and third-order consequences

**People who over weigh the first-order consequences of their decisions and ignore the effects of subsequent-order consequences rarely reach their goals.**

This is because first-order consequences often have opposite desirabilities from subsequent-order consequences:

- The first-order consequence of exercising → pain and time spent.
- The second-order consequence exercising → better health and more attractive appearance.

People who choose what they really want, avoid temptations, and get over the pains that drive them away from what they really want, are much more likely to have successful lives.

### Own your outcomes

Life gives your so many decisions to make and so many opportunities to recover from your mistakes that, if you handle them well, you can have a terrific life. Sometimes there are major influences on the quality of our lives that come from things beyond our control, but for the most part even the worst circumstances can be made better with the right approach.

Whatever circumstances life brings you, you will be more likely to succeed and find happiness if you take responsibility for making your decisions well instead of complaining about things being beyond your control.

**It's up to you to connect what you want with what you need to do to get it and then find the courage to carry it through.**

### Look at the machine from the higher level

Higher-level thinking gives you the ability to study and influence the cause-effect relationships at play in your life and use them to get the outcomes you want.

**Think of yourself as a machine operating within a machine and know that you have the ability to alter your machines to produce better outcomes.**

Let's call that way you will operate to achieve your goals your machine. It consists of a design:

- The things that have to get done.
- The people who will do the things that need to get done. (You and those who help you)

It is important to put the right people in the right place, they need different qualities to do their jobs well so that the machine will produce the outcomes you seek.

**By comparing your outcomes with your goals, you can determine how to modify your machine.**

**Distinguish between you as the designer of your machine and you as the worker within your machine.**

- If you can recognize the differences between those roles and that it is much more important that you are a good designer/manager of your life than a good worker in it, you will be on the right path. **Be objective about what the "worker you' is really like.**

**The biggest mistake most people make is to not see themselves and others objectively, which leads them to bump into their own and others' weaknesses again and again.**

**Successful people are those who can go above themselves to see things objectively and manage those things to shape change.**

- They can take in the perspectives of others instead of being trapped in their own heads with their own biases.
- They are able to look objectively at themselves, and at others, and adjust their role in order to have them succeed at achieving their goals.
- When encountering your weaknesses you have four choices:
  1. You can deny them
  2. You can accept them and work at them in order to try to convert them to strengths
  3. You can accept your weaknesses and find ways around them
  4. You can change what you are going after.

**Asking others who are strong in areas where you are weak to help you is a must-develop skill that will prevent you from doing what you shouldn't be doing.**

**If you are open-minded enough and determined, you can get virtually anything you want.**

1. Don't confuse what you wish were true with what is really true.
2. Don't worry about looking good—worry instead about achieving your goals.
3. Don't over weigh first-order consequence relative to subsequent-order ones.
4. Don't let pain stand in the way of progress.
5. Don't blame bad outcomes on anyone but yourself.

## Chapter Two: Use the 5-Step Process to Get What You Want Out of Life

The personal evolutionary process take place in five distinct steps. 1) Have clear goals. 2) Identify and don't tolerate the problems that stand in the way of you achieving those goals. 3) Accurately diagnose those problems to get at their root causes. 4) Design plans that will get you around them. 5) Do what's necessary to push these designs through to results.

**Do these steps one at a time and in order;** When setting goals: just set goals; when you are diagnosing problems: just diagnose them. And approach this process in a clearheaded manner, looking down on yourself from a higher level and being ruthlessly honest.

To help yourself stay centered and effective, pretend that your life is a martial art or a game, accept its rules and you'll get used to its' discomfort.

**So what if you don't have all the skills you need to succeed?** You just have to know when they are needed and where you can go to get them.

### Have clear goals

**Prioritize: while you can have virtually anything you want, you can't have everything you want.**

**Don't Confuse Goals with Desires.**

- A proper goal is something that you really need to achieve. Typically, desires are first-order consequences.

**Decide what you really want in life by reconciling your goals and your desires.**

- Without passion, life would be dull; But what's key is what you do with your passion.
- What will ultimately fulfill you are things that feel right at both levels, both desires _and_ goals.

**Don't mistake the trappings of success with success itself.**

**Never rule out a goal because you think it's unattainable.**

- What you think is attainable is just a function of what you know at the moment.

**Remember that great expectations create great capabilities.**

**Almost nothing can stop you if you have a) flexibility and b) self-accountability**

- Flexibility is what allows you to accept what reality teaches you; self-accountability is essential because if you really believe that failing to achieve a goal is your personal failure, you will see your failing to achieve it as indicative that you haven't been creative or flexible or determined enough to do what it takes. And you will be much more motivated to find the way.

**Knowing how to deal well with your setbacks is as important as knowing how to move forward.**

- Your mission is to always make the best possible choices, knowing that you will be rewarded if you do.

### Identify and don't tolerate problems

**View painful problems as potential improvements that are screaming at you.**

**Don't avoid confronting problems because they are rooted in harsh realities that are unpleasant to look at.**

- Thinking about problems that are difficult to solve may make you anxious, but _not_ thinking about them should make you more anxious still.
- **Acknowledging your weaknesses is not the same as surrendering to them**

**Be specific in identifying your problems.**

- You need to be precise, because different problems have different solutions.

**Don't mistake a cause of a problem with the real problem.**

- "I can't get enough sleep" is not a problem; it is a potential cause of a problem. To clarify your thinking, try to identify the bad outcome first.

**Distinguish big problems from small ones.**

**Once you identify a problem don't tolerate it.**

- Tolerating a problem has the same consequences as failing to identify it.
- You need to develop a fierce intolerance of badness of any kind, regardless of its severity.

### Diagnose problems to get at their root causes

**Focus on the "what is" before deciding "what to do about it."**

- Strategic thinking requires both:

  **Diagnosis**

  **_and..._**

  **Design**

  It involves speaking with relevant people and looking at the evidence together to determine the root causes. Like principles, root causes manifest themselves over and over again in seemingly different situations.

**Distinguish proximate causes from root causes.**

- Proximate causes are typically the actions (or lack of actions) that lead to problems, so they are described with verbs.
- Root causes run much deeper and they are typically described with adjectives.

**Recognize that knowing what someone (including you) is like will tell you what you can expect from them.**

- People almost always find it difficult to identify and accept their own mistakes and weaknesses. You all need to get over this.

### Design a plan

**Go back before you go forward.**

- Replay the story of where you have been (or what you have done) that led up to where you are now, and then visualize what you and others must do in the future so you will reach their goals.

**Think about your problem as a set of outcomes produced by a machine.**

**Remember that there are typically many paths to achieving your goals.**

**Think of your plan as being like a move script in that you visualize who will do what through time.**

**Write down your plan for everyone to see and to measure your progress against.**

- The tasks, the narrative, and the goals are different, so don't mix them up. Remember, the tasks are what connect the narrative to your goals.

**Recognize that it doesn't take a long time to design a good plan; Designing precedes doing!**

### Push through to completion

**Great planners who don't execute their plans go nowhere.**

**Good work habits are vastly underrated.**

**Establish clear metrics to make certain that you are following your plan.**

- Your values determine your goals. If the process is working, your goals will change more slowly than your designs, which will change more slowly than your tasks.
- The first three steps are synthesizing (knowing where you want to go and what's really going on). Designing solutions and making sure that the designs are implemented are shaping.

### Remember that weaknesses don't matter if you find solutions

_Have humility so that you can get what you want from others._

**Look at the patterns of your mistakes and identify at which step in the 5-step process you typically fail.**

**Everyone has at least one big thing that stands in the way of their success; find yours and deal with it (Write it down, and why it exists)**

### Understand your own and others' mental maps and humility

- Humility can be even more valuable than having good mental maps if it leads you to seek better answers than you could come up with on your own.

## Chapter Three: Be Radically Open-Minded

This chapter explains how to get around the two things standing in most people's way of getting what they want out of life. These barriers exist because of the way that our brain works, so nearly everyone encounters them.

### Recognize your two barriers

1. **Understand your _Ego Barrier._**
   - This barrier is your subliminal defense mechanism that makes it hard for you to accept your mistakes and weaknesses.
2. **Understand your _Blind Spot Barrier_**
   - In addition to your ego barrier, you also have blind spots. You're hard-headed, and this prevents you from seeing things accurately,

Also, **Understand the two "Yous" fighting to control you.**

You have a higher level "you" and a low level "you", and they like to fight.

This conflict is universal; Someone asking themselves "Why did I do _a_ and not _b,_" it is because they let their lower-level selves win over their high-level selves.

> To be effective, you must not let your need to be right be more important than your need to find out what's True.

Differences in thinking can be symbiotic and complementary instead of disruptive; practice radical open-mindedness, and you can achieve the ability to:

- Teach your brain to work in a way that doesn't come naturally.
- Use compensating mechanisms (Reminders, Notes)
- Rely on the help of others who are strong where they are weak.

### Practice Radical Open-Mindedness

This means,

...holding a genuine worry that you might not be seeing your choices optimally.

...being able to effectively explore different points of view.

...replacing your attachment to always being right with the joy of learning what's true.

**Humility,** is what helps your upper-level you see and consider all the good choices and make the best possible decisions.
