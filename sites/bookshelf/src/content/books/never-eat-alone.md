## The Mindset

### Becoming a Member of the Club

Building your network must not be a selfish endeavor, you should build your network by building other people's networks. The success of others will mean your success as well.

**Building a career, and a life,** with the help and support of friends and family and associates has some incredible virtues:

- It's _never_ boring. You'll learn more about yourself, other people. business, and the world.
- Everyone benefits from network effects, the company you work for, your peers, and yourself.
- Connecting is easier than ever in our networked world. Use this to your advantage.

> Poverty, I realized, wasn't only a lack of financial resources, it was isolation from the kind of people that could help you make more of yourself.

### Don't Keep Score, Don't deny generosity

In a room of people, especially under a business context, everyone in the room will be asking for, or willing to help. Therefore, you should become as willing to ask for help as your are to get it.

### What's your Mission?

Have you ever sat down and thought seriously about what you love? What you're truly good at? What you want to accomplish in life? What obstacles are in the way? There a few things you can do to answer these questions: **Gather good information, putting goals to paper, create your board of advisors...**

#### Gather good information

##### Look _Inside_

Find some time for yourself away from constraints, create a list of your dreams and goals, putting anything and everything down. Next to that first list, write down in a second column all the things that bring you joy: the people, places, hobbies, and things that you want to spend more time with or more time doing.

##### Look _Outside_

After you've set yourself up, ask the people who know you best what your strengths and weaknesses are. What they admire about you and what they suggest you get help from others for.

> Disciplined dreamers all have one thing in common: a mission. But, the transformation of a dream into reality requires hard work.

##### Putting goals to paper

The **Network Action Plan,** separated in three parts, this plan will help you connect your goals to the people who can help you best achieve them.

The first part of the plan: Develop the goals that will help you fulfill your mission.

- Make a list of what you'd like to accomplish three years from today. Then, work backward in both one-year and three-month increments to develop short- and, mid-term goals. Under each time frame, create and 'A' and 'B' goal that will contribute to where you want to be in three years.

The second part of the plan: Connect your developed goals to the people that can help you.

- Using the same process as the first part of the plan, connect each of the 'A' and 'B' goals, to people.

The third part of the plan: Determine the best way to reach out to those people.

- [In my case] I should look to Denver Devs, local meetups, friends, students, and professors.

**The Purpose of this exercise is to show that the is a _process_, involved in building a network.**

##### Create a personal "Board of Advisors"

With your plan in place, seek out the people who will help you stay focused, find mentors, an old friend or two.

#### Build It Before You Need It

> In reality, people who have the largest circle of contacts, mentors, and friends know that you must reach out to others long before you need anything at all.

#### The Genius of Audactiy

Mastering the audacity to talk with people who you don't know comes down to balancing your fear against the fear of failure and its repercussions.

There are a few things you can do to make good on this commitment and get more comfortable being uncomfortable.

- **Find a role model**
  - Find your friends who are social butterflies and emulate them, have them show you how to meet people.
- Learn to speak
- **Get Involved**
  - You'll feel more comfortable when you surround yourself with others doing the same thing, and share your enthusiasm.
- **Get Therapy**
- **JUST DO IT.**
  - Make it a goal to talk to one new person each week. You'll find that it gets easier with practice.

    > Fail, fail again. Fail better. | _Fear debilitates_.
